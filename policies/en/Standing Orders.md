<h1 align="center">Standing Orders</h1>


### § 1 Purpose of the Standing Orders

1. These standing orders govern the internal working procedures of the association, in particular:
    - the fundamental principles of cooperation and decision-making,
    - the establishment, structure, and interaction of the association's subdivisions,
    - the conduct of assemblies,
    - the documentation and the adoption of governance documents.

2. They give further shape to the statutes, in particular § 5a, without extending or restricting them.


### § 2 Fundamental Principles of Cooperation

1. The association acts according to the following principles:
    - personal responsibility before control,
    - transparency before hierarchy,
    - simplicity before complexity.

2. Delegation is always bound to a specific purpose, limited in time or substance, and revocable. Further details are regulated by § 8 of the board order.

3. There is no authority to issue instructions between members outside of explicitly granted mandates.

4. Pursuant to § 5a para. 4 of the statutes, decisions are made in accordance with the designated, documented procedures and not at the discretion of individual persons.


### § 3 Decision-Making Processes

1. Decisions should be made, as far as possible, where the greatest subject-matter proximity exists.

2. Fundamental decisions within the meaning of § 5a para. 3 of the statutes concern in particular:
    - the strategic direction,
    - major financial commitments,
    - changes to existing structures.

3. Major financial and resource decisions are those which, relative to the funds available, have a significant effect on liquidity. They require approval by the general assembly or the members' council. The decisive reference is the funds of the administrative unit that disposes of them.

4. Operational decisions may be made by mandated bodies and must be documented in a comprehensible manner.

5. Where responsibility is unclear, the decision is referred to the members' council or the general assembly.


### § 4 Subdivisions and Administrative Units

1. Pursuant to § 5a para. 1 of the statutes, the association organizes itself decentrally. Its subdivisions are projects, working groups, and branches.

2. Subdivisions are independent, self-administering units of the association. They are neither separate legal entities nor organs of the association and act on the basis of a granted mandate.

3. Subdivisions are subject to the same obligations towards the general assembly as the board, in particular the transparency, notification, and accountability obligations under § 5a of the statutes.

4. Subdivisions organize themselves within the scope of their mandate, define their own objectives and responsibilities, and document their activity in a comprehensible manner.

5. Every subdivision designates a responsible contact person towards the next higher administrative unit.

6. Decisions of fundamental importance within the meaning of § 5a para. 3 of the statutes must be submitted to the general assembly or the members' council.

7. The granting body holds a right of veto over decisions of a subdivision. Further details are regulated by the procedural order.

8. Administrative units exist at federal, state, district, and local level. Where responsibility cannot be clearly determined, the federal level is responsible.

9. The administrative office is the executive administrative unit of its level, established by board resolution. It is not a separate organ and acts on behalf of and under the professional direction of the board of its level. At federal level this is the federal administrative office.


### § 5 Projects

1. A project is a substantively delimited undertaking for the fulfillment of the association's purposes.

2. The project request defines at least:
    - the project definition, including its relation to the purposes of the association,
    - the project scope in substantive, temporal, and financial terms,
    - the project lead.

3. The request must be addressed to the next responsible administrative unit; in case of doubt, to the federal level.

4. The board of the level addressed decides on the request pursuant to § 9 para. 2 of the board order. Upon approval, the project receives a mandate conferring the operational powers required for its implementation.

5. Further details are regulated by the procedural order.


### § 6 Working Groups

1. A working group is a permanent, functionally delimited subdivision within an administrative unit.

2. Within the scope of its mandate, a working group performs the tasks of its functional area, in particular:
    - the granting of mandates within its area of responsibility,
    - the regulation of administrative responsibilities,
    - the supervision of the processes and resources assigned to it,
    - acting as the contact point for the corresponding functional area of subordinate administrative units.

3. The request to establish a working group must be addressed to the next responsible administrative unit; it is decided upon pursuant to § 9 para. 2 of the board order.

4. Where a working group goes beyond the complexity of a project, it may adopt its own orders, hold its own assemblies, and elect its own board; § 7 paras. 5 and 6 as well as § 9 of these standing orders apply accordingly.


### § 7 Branches

1. A branch is a regionally responsible subdivision of the federal association. It is not a separate association but a non-independent branch of the federal association.

2. Branches exist as state associations, district associations, or local associations.

3. A branch organizes itself within the framework of the statutes. It may adopt its own branch statutes and its own orders, hold assemblies, and elect its own board.

4. The board of the branch is the contact point towards the next higher administrative unit.

5. The organization of a branch must not differ structurally from that of the federal association. In particular, the structure of organs under § 5 of the statutes, the principle of decentralization under § 5a of the statutes, and the binding effect of procedures under § 5a para. 4 of the statutes apply accordingly.

6. Within the scope of their mandate, branches grant mandates independently; § 8 of the board order applies accordingly. They document such mandates and report them to the next higher administrative unit and to the general assembly.


### § 8 Founding a Branch

1. The establishment of a branch requires:
    - state association: at least 10 members of the respective federal state from at least 7 different regions broadly distributed across that state,
    - district association: at least 7 members from the entire district area,
    - local association: at least 3 members from the locality.

2. The intention to establish a branch must be resolved at a proper founding assembly. That assembly resolves at least:
    - the branch statutes,
    - the election of the board of the branch,
    - the minutes of the assembly.

3. The minutes must be signed by all participants and finally by the chair of the assembly.

4. The signed minutes, including the adopted branch statutes, must be sent to the state board or, where none exists, to the federal board.

5. From receipt onwards, the board addressed is obliged to decide on the request pursuant to § 9 para. 2 of the board order and, where the requirements are met, to grant a mandate.

6. The handling of formal defects and the further procedure are governed by the procedural order.


### § 9 Branch Statutes and Internal Register

1. Branch statutes may regulate exclusively:
    - the processes of self-administration of the branch, insofar as these are compatible with the statutes and the orders of the federal association,
    - the scope of its responsibility at state, district, or local level.

2. Branch statutes and orders of a branch do not apply at federal level. They serve exclusively the decentralized self-administration of the respective subdivision.

3. They must be deposited with the internal register of the association. Amendments must be reported to the federal administrative office or the federal board.

4. Where a provision contradicts the statutes or the orders of the federal association, the higher-ranking provision prevails; the conflicting provision is invalid.

5. Paragraphs 2 to 4 apply accordingly to orders of working groups under § 6 para. 4.


### § 10 Funds of Subdivisions

1. State associations are entitled to open their own independent bank account for the association. They choose their business bank themselves.

2. For the account it maintains, the state association grants subaccounts to the branches and other subdivisions subordinate to it within its area of responsibility on its own responsibility.

3. All other subdivisions may request the next higher administrative unit responsible for them to set up their own subaccount; where no such unit exists, the federal administrative office or the federal board.

4. Bank powers of attorney are granted exclusively by the board maintaining the account.

5. Subdivisions may collect donations for their project or working group and dispose of them freely within the framework of the statutes.

6. Subdivisions may apply for funding from the next higher administrative unit responsible for them.

7. The disposal of funds is subject to §§ 2a and 2b of the statutes, to the transparency and accountability obligations under § 5a of the statutes, and to the approval requirement under § 3 para. 3.


### § 11 Assemblies

1. Assemblies may take place physically, digitally, or in hybrid form.

2. The moderator is determined at the beginning of the assembly and has the following responsibilities:
    - structuring the discussion,
    - ensuring fair participation,
    - maintaining focus on the objective.

3. Contributions should be brief, factual, and solution-oriented. The assembly may set speaking time limits.

4. These provisions apply accordingly to assemblies of subdivisions.


### § 12 Preparation of Resolutions

1. Resolution proposals should contain:
    - the objective of the decision,
    - a brief justification,
    - a concrete decision option.

2. Complex topics should be documented in advance and made accessible in the members' area.

3. The aim is to make decisions in an informed but efficient manner.


### § 13 Documentation and Transparency

1. All material decisions must be documented.

2. Documentation is maintained digitally in the members' area, in a comprehensible and permanently accessible form.

3. Documentation covers in particular resolutions, responsibilities, granted mandates, and ongoing projects.

4. Where confidentiality obligations under § 5a para. 5 of the statutes stand in the way, publication is made in anonymized form.


### § 14 Governance Repository

1. The association maintains its governance documents in a publicly accessible version control repository.

2. The repository is divided into at least the following branches:
    - `main` – the adopted versions of the governance documents,
    - `unadopted` – the maintained state of the documents prior to their adoption,
    - `policies/*` – the development of new orders until their transfer into `unadopted`,
    - `statutes` – the development of the statutes,
    - `cps` – the development of the Certification Practice Statements.

3. The `main` branch must be secured by branch protection.

4. Branches of the association, as well as working groups that go beyond the complexity of a project, are obliged in the interest of traceability to maintain their own governance repository modelled on that of the federal association.


### § 15 Adoption of Governance Documents

1. A resolved version is adopted exclusively by merging a pull request into the `main` branch.

2. The merge must always be carried out as a merge commit. Other methods, in particular squash and rebase merges, are impermissible.

3. The description of the merge commit must contain:
    - the date of the resolution,
    - a validation link to the digitally signed assembly minutes,
    - for pull requests from the `statutes` branch, additionally the date of the register notification.

4. Before being merged, every pull request requires review and confirmation by at least one further member who did not submit the pull request themselves.

5. The reviewer confirms:
    - that the version to be merged corresponds to the resolved version,
    - the accuracy and completeness of the information under paragraph 3,
    - the correct resolution of any merge conflicts.

6. When resolving merge conflicts, precise attention must be paid to the correct transfer of the resolved version.

7. Circumventing branch protection by administrators of the organization is strictly prohibited for all pull requests that do not originate from the `cps` branch.

8. Violations of paragraphs 1 to 7 must be documented in the board file; the affected adoption must be brought into a proper state without delay.


### § 16 Digital Systems

1. The association uses digital systems for communication, voting, and documentation.

2. These systems must be comprehensible, as data-minimizing as possible, and accessible to all members.

3. Digital identity and voting procedures must comply with the requirements of the statutes.


### § 17 Conflict Resolution

1. Conflicts should first be resolved directly between the parties involved.

2. If this is not possible, a neutral mediator may be appointed or the members' council may be involved.

3. The remedies under § 10a of the statutes and the procedural order remain unaffected.

4. The aim is always a solution-oriented resolution in accordance with the purpose of the association.


### § 18 Entry into Force and Amendments

1. These standing orders, as well as any amendments, enter into force upon resolution by the general assembly.

2. They may be amended within the framework of the statutes to respond to internal and external developments.
