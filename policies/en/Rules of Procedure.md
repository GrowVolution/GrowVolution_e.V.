<h1 align="center">Rules of Procedure</h1>


### § 1 Purpose of the Rules of Procedure

1. These rules of procedure govern the internal operations of the association, in particular:
    - procedures of assemblies,
    - structure and cooperation of working groups,
    - operational decision-making processes.

2. They specify the statutes, in particular §5a, without extending or restricting them.


### § 2 Principles of Collaboration

1. The association acts according to the following principles:
    - self-responsibility over control
    - transparency over hierarchy
    - simplicity over complexity

2. Delegation is always:
    - purpose-bound,
    - limited in time or scope,
    - revocable by the general assembly or the members’ council.

3. There is no authority to issue instructions between members outside explicitly granted mandates.


### § 3 Working Groups and Projects

1. Working groups or projects are formed to implement the purposes of the association.

2. These:
    - organize themselves independently,
    - define their goals and responsibilities themselves,
    - document their activities transparently.

3. Each working group appoints a responsible contact person.

4. Working groups may:
    - submit proposals,
    - make decisions within the scope of their mandate.

5. Decisions of fundamental importance must be submitted to the general assembly or the members’ council.


### § 4 Decision-Making Processes

1. Decisions should be made as close as possible to the relevant subject matter.

2. Fundamental decisions include in particular:
    - strategic direction,
    - major financial commitments,
    - changes to existing structures.

3. Operational decisions:
    - may be made by working groups or mandated individuals,
    - must be documented in a comprehensible manner.

4. In case of uncertainty regarding responsibility:
   → the decision shall be referred to the members’ council or the general assembly.


### § 5 Assemblies

1. Assemblies may be held physically, digitally, or in hybrid form.

2. A moderator is appointed at the beginning of the assembly.

3. The moderator has the following tasks:
    - structuring the discussion,
    - ensuring fair participation,
    - maintaining focus on objectives.

4. Contributions should be:
    - concise,
    - factual,
    - solution-oriented.

5. The assembly may set limits on speaking time.


### § 6 Preparation of Resolutions

1. Draft resolutions should include:
    - the objective of the decision,
    - a brief justification,
    - a concrete decision option.

2. Complex topics should be:
    - documented in advance,
    - made accessible in the members’ area.

3. The aim is to make informed but efficient decisions.


### § 7 Documentation and Transparency

1. All significant decisions must be documented.

2. Documentation shall be:
    - maintained digitally in the members’ area,
    - comprehensible and permanently accessible.

3. In particular, the following shall be documented:
    - resolutions,
    - responsibilities,
    - ongoing projects.


### § 8 Digital Systems

1. The association uses digital systems for:
    - communication,
    - voting,
    - documentation.

2. These systems must be:
    - transparent,
    - as data-efficient as possible,
    - accessible to all members.

3. Digital identity and voting procedures must comply with the requirements of the statutes.


### § 9 Conflict Resolution

1. Conflicts should first be resolved directly between the parties involved.

2. If this is not possible:
    - a neutral mediator may be appointed, or
    - the members’ council may be involved.

3. The goal is always a solution-oriented resolution in line with the purpose of the association.


### § 10 Entry into Force and Amendments

1. These rules of procedure, as well as any amendments, enter into force upon resolution by the general assembly.

2. They may be amended within the framework of the statutes to respond to internal and external developments.
